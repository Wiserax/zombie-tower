# Visual prototype contract

## What this pass must answer

Can a mobile browser show a satisfying, readable siege with several hundred independent-looking humanoids, while leaving enough performance headroom for the eventual game?

The player watches one central bastion. Six approach streams contain ordinary shamblers, lean faster runners, and larger hunched brutes. The camera remains stable during play. Enemy density is the spectacle; text overlays remain brief and small.

## Reference observations

The supplied They Are Billions clips use crowded fronts, irregular silhouettes, grounded shadows, industrial defenses, restrained terrain colors, and bright short-lived impacts. A killing blow opens a visible gap in the pack. Replacing enemies with dots, instantly deleting bodies, or filling the screen with damage numbers would lose that effect.

The Tower footage establishes the stationary defense silhouette and simultaneous automatic weapon systems. Its long-term economy, upgrade rules, and session structure are intentionally outside this visual pass. The supplied workbook is a research input, not the prototype's shipped balance.

## Implemented attack language

| Attack | Readable motion | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Twin gun batteries | Rotating barrels, warm narrow tracer, muzzle flash | Small hit flash and stagger |
| Crossbow batteries | Three forward-moving piercing bolts | Multiple bodies along a line |
| Tesla core | Cyan branching ribbons from an elevated coil | White core flash, brief stun, blue discharge |
| Mortars | Warm shell follows a visible high arc | Fire flash, expanding pressure ring, dark smoke, debris, pack knockback |
| Overcharge | Large expanding electrical pulse | A substantial gap in the horde; bounded electrical bursts and falling bodies |

Tower details include masonry courses, iron ribs, copper coils, boiler pipes, steam, ammo crates, signal pennants, and warm perimeter lamps. Terrain is original procedural ground with ruins, rocks, trees and scattered debris.

## Scope limits

This is not a They Are Billions asset recreation or a finished The Tower clone. It has no shop, research progression, saves, monetization, chapter selection, or final economy. Chapter-styled UI demonstrates the intended Firstfire continuity. Automatic lab resets keep the visual scene available without pretending that survival is balanced.

Before the full game, get a human verdict on crowd density, tower readability, impact satisfaction, and portrait framing. A performance result does not establish that the visuals are good enough.
